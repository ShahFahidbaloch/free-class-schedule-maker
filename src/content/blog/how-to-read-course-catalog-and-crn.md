# How to Read a Course Catalog and CRN Numbers

When you log into your university's course registration system for the first time—whether it runs on Ellucian Banner, PeopleSoft, Workday Student, or a proprietary campus portal—the screen looks less like a friendly academic planner and more like an encrypted aviation manifest.

Abbreviations like `CRN 74819`, `PREREQ: MATH 112 w/ C or higher`, `CO-REQ: CHEM 101L`, `LEC vs REC vs LAB`, and `RESTRICTED: SO/JR/SR ONLY` crowd the table columns.

Understanding this specialized registrar vocabulary is not just academic trivia—it is the difference between securing the classes you need to graduate on time or having your registration rejected at 8:00 AM because of an unfulfilled prerequisite error.

## 1. What is a CRN (Course Reference Number)?

The most critical alphanumeric code on your registration screen is the **CRN** (Course Reference Number, sometimes labeled as *Class Number*, *Call Number*, or *Section ID*).

A CRN is a unique, institution-wide identifier (typically 5 numeric digits, such as `83921`) assigned to one **specific offering** of a course in a given academic term.

### Course Code vs. CRN: Understanding the Crucial Difference
Students frequently confuse the general **Course Code** with the specific **CRN**:
- **Course Code (e.g., `PSYC 101`):** Represents the abstract subject curriculum: *Introduction to Psychology*. The university might offer ten different sections of PSYC 101 this semester, taught by three different professors at different times of day.
- **CRN (e.g., `14892`):** Represents the exact, concrete section meeting on *Monday/Wednesday/Friday from 10:00 AM to 10:50 AM in Campbell Hall Room 212, taught by Professor Davis*.

When your registration window opens, registrar systems do not ask you to type `"Introduction to Psychology"`. They provide a fast-entry row of blank boxes where you enter the **exact 5-digit CRNs**. Entering the wrong CRN could accidentally enroll you in a 7:00 PM evening section on a remote satellite campus!

## 2. Deciphering the Course Numbering Hierarchy

University courses are categorized by standard numeric tiers that signal academic rigor, prerequisite expectations, and audience level:

| Course Level | Typical Numbering | Academic Audience | Description & Expectations |
| :---: | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Remedial / Developmental** | `001 – 099` | Pre-Collegiate | Non-credit or developmental skills courses (e.g., Pre-Algebra, ESL) designed to prepare students for college-level work. |
| **Lower-Division (Freshman)** | `100 – 199` | 1st Year Undergraduates | Introductory survey courses (e.g., `HIST 101`, `BIOL 110`). Broad conceptual overviews with minimal prerequisites. |
| **Lower-Division (Sophomore)** | `200 – 299` | 2nd Year Undergraduates | Foundational major courses (e.g., `CHEM 201 Organic Chemistry`, `MATH 240 Linear Algebra`). Often require 100-level prerequisites. |
| **Upper-Division (Junior/Senior)** | `300 – 499` | 3rd & 4th Year Majors | Advanced, specialized seminars and research studios (e.g., `ECON 450 Econometrics`). Restricted to students with declared majors. |
| **Graduate Level** | `500 – 799+` | Master's & Doctoral | Rigorous graduate-only seminars. Undergraduates require formal departmental permission to enroll. |

## 3. Prerequisite vs. Co-Requisite: What Every Student Must Know

Few registration errors are more stressful than the dreaded red banner:  
`"Registration Failed: Prerequisite and Test Score Error."`

Understanding prerequisite rules prevents this disaster:

### 1. Prerequisite (PREREQ)
A course, test score (SAT/ACT/AP), or placement tier you must **successfully complete in a prior semester** before you are permitted to enroll.
- *Example:* `MATH 141 Calculus I` is a prerequisite for `MATH 142 Calculus II`.
- *Grade Minimums:* Most departments require a minimum grade of **C or C+** in a prerequisite course. Earning a D or D- usually does not unlock subsequent waterfall courses.

### 2. Co-Requisite (COREQ)
A complementary course component that must be taken **simultaneously in the exact same semester**.
- *Example:* Enrolling in `PHYS 201 Physics Lecture` strictly requires simultaneous enrollment in `PHYS 201L Physics Laboratory`. If you add the lecture without adding the lab CRN in the same submission, the registrar portal will reject both.

### 3. Concurrent Enrollment Allowed
Some departments allow a prerequisite to be taken concurrently if space permits. Check your institution's specific catalog footnotes.

## 4. Instructional Delivery Modes: LEC, REC, LAB, SEM, DIS

University courses often split instructional time across multiple meeting formats:

- **LEC (Lecture):** Large auditorium meetings (often 100 to 400 students) where the primary professor delivers core conceptual material. Meets 2 to 3 times per week.
- **REC (Recitation) or DIS (Discussion):** Smaller breakout groups (20 to 30 students) led by a Graduate Teaching Assistant (TA). Focuses on solving homework problem sets, asking questions, and reviewing for exams. Often mandatory for attendance!
- **LAB (Laboratory):** Hands-on scientific experimentation, engineering design, or computer programming in specialized facilities. Meets in extended 2-to-3 hour blocks.
- **SEM (Seminar):** Small, discussion-intensive classes (12 to 18 students) focused on analyzing scholarly papers, debates, and presentations. Common in upper-division humanities and honors programs.

To ensure a seamless, panic-free registration appointment:

1. **Verify Your Priority Registration Time:** Registration windows open in staggered waves based on earned credits, athlete status, or honors program standing. Note your exact date and hour.
2. **Clear All Administrative Holds:** Unpaid parking tickets, overdue library books, missing immunization records, or pending academic advising holds will block your registration portal. Clear all holds at least one week prior!
